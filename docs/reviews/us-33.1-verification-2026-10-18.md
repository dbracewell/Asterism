## Initial failures and repairs

The first browser invocation was blocked by the sandbox's local-port binding
restriction (`EPERM` on loopback port 3000); it did not execute tests. The same
isolated runner executed with permission to open its local test servers.

The first executed focused run had **17 passes and 2 failures**, without retries:
Latte's faded sidebar label was 3.37:1, One Dark's faded label was 4.32:1 and its
secondary hover was 4.24:1. Sidebar group labels now retain their full semantic
foreground; secondary hover darkens its surface instead of blending toward the
foreground. Assertions and thresholds were retained. The expanded rerun passed
all 19 cases. The switch-thumb adaptation also prevents a dark theme's light
unchecked track from sharing its thumb color; the full run checks both states.

The subsequent input-role audit found additional text-bearing consumers of the
stronger boundary token: outline badges, appearance selectors, model-picker menus
and the chat composer. Those now use background, popover or muted fills. Badge
links retain opaque focus, the model filter uses the shared Input, and the composer
container exposes its boundary and focus ring. Actual production consumers are
included in the palette harness rather than testing replacement markup.

An intermediate consumer-harness run had **11 passes and 8 failures** because an
unscoped `getByRole("combobox")` assertion matched the newly added appearance
selectors as well as its intended model picker. The assertion now targets the
original picker; expected behavior and all contrast thresholds are unchanged.

## Scope and limits

This verifies the bundled defaults and reviewed component states, not whole-app
accessibility conformance. Custom overrides keep their saved colors and are not
rewritten or certified. Feature-specific semantic colors, syntax highlighting,
typography and reduced motion remain US-33.2 work; a wider reusable component/state
matrix and explicit CI integration remain US-33.3 work.

No backend/API schema, generated client, dependency or lockfile changes were
needed. Tests use disposable storage; the working database is untouched. No
remote CI, push, deployment or merge is claimed.
